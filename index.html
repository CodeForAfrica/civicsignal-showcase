Why this matters now

Africa's information ecosystem is moving faster than any one-time map can capture.

This isn't a CivicSignal talking point — it's what the world's largest annual study of news consumption found this year. Code for Africa is a named sponsor of the Reuters Institute's Digital News Report 2026, and CfA's Chris Roper co-authored its South Africa chapter.

A second, newer footprint

Legacy and online media is our foundation layer. We are adding an emerging media layer.

MediaData and MediaCloud, above, map and monitor Africa's established news industry — licensed outlets, broadcasters, news agencies. But a growing share of African news and commentary now happens outside that industry entirely: independent digital-native publishers, individual creator-journalists, and newsletters, Telegram channels and fediverse accounts that never touch a crawlable website. Wavelength is CivicSignal's monitoring footprint for that emerging layer — three source types, unified into one live view.

GIZ SRIEWA partnership · 2026 report

Media Capture: How foreign states infiltrate media in francophone West Africa

The second in CivicSignal's series tracing foreign state influence in African media. It documents how state-linked entities drew outlets and journalists in Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal and Niger into relationships that embedded foreign narratives, distorted editorial practice, and sidelined citizen concerns.

  • Of 23 Ivorian outlets reviewed, only one published editorial guidelines and none had a corrections policy — despite Côte d'Ivoire ranking among Africa's highest for Journalism Trust Initiative uptake. Senegal did modestly better, from a low base.
  • Russia gave Linfodrome, a private Ivorian outlet, exclusive access to Russian ambassadors (8 interviews, versus 1 for the state press agency) and had it republish 31+ undisclosed Sputnik articles.
  • Senegal's state news agency removed articles critical of Chinese interests, despite carrying Xinhua's logo as a listed partner.
  • China's "La Chine à mes yeux" awards drew Côte d'Ivoire's most prominent outlets — including the state press agency and public broadcaster — into sustained, rewarded pro-China coverage.
  • A Nigerien outlet, ActuNiger, ran paid communications for a Russian-linked NGO and wrote 66 articles about it; a Russian propaganda network republished 93 of its articles.

Produced with funding from GIZ (Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit) under the "Strengthening Resilient Information Ecosystems in West Africa" (SRIEWA) project.

Peer-reviewed · AJIC, No. 34 (2024)

Healthcare research and development (R&D) as reflected in digital news: Analysis from Kenya, Nigeria, and South Africa

Published in The African Journal of Information and Communication by Emily Boardman Ndulue (Media Ecosystems Analysis Group) with Code for Africa's Sandra Roberts and Praise Ocheanya Abraham. The study filtered a corpus of over 5 million articles down to 2,917 relevant pieces from 377 publications using CivicSignal's MediaCloud infrastructure, combining computational NLP with manual qualitative analysis.

  • Healthcare R&D drew an overall low level of news attention — under 0.1% of stories across the three countries' news ecosystems.
  • Coverage spiked around medical education programmes, high-profile figures and breakthrough innovations, rather than sustained R&D reporting.
  • HIV/AIDS, COVID-19, cancer, maternal health and mental health were the most frequently covered conditions; medications themselves received minimal attention.
  • About 15% of relevant articles involved technological or digital health interventions, with AI appearing prominently; government officials, researchers and bodies like the WHO were the most-quoted stakeholders.

DOI: 10.23962/ajic.i34.20161. A demonstration of the same MediaCloud/CivicSignal infrastructure behind Wavelength, applied to a specific research question.
