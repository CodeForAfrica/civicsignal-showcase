What happens when the funding disappears.

#MediaMeltdown is CivicSignal Research's ongoing series tracking how community media across Africa is coping with a decade of shrinking advertising, donor and grant revenue — and now, sudden US foreign-assistance cuts. Four posts published so far, summarised below with links to the originals.

The series

Four posts, one continuing story.

Each post draws on the same #MediaMeltdown survey work — community media organisations across Africa, surveyed directly about their finances, staffing and strategy.

18 August 2025 · Sandra Roberts

US funding cuts shake entire media and information ecosystems

A #MediaMeltdown survey of 51 organisations across 12 African countries found 31 had experienced disruptions after US foreign-assistance freezes — fact-checkers and support intermediaries hit hardest. The 26 affected organisations reported 85 distinct impacts between them: project timeline disruptions (22 orgs), cancelled projects (16 orgs), and abandoned training programmes (12 orgs). The loss of an estimated $268 million in annual USAID media-development funding has weakened reporting infrastructure and knowledge networks continent-wide, creating conditions for disinformation to spread further.

7 August 2025 · Nomshado Lubisi

How community media is reinventing itself

Drawing on the same 2025 survey of 42 organisations across 11 countries, this post examines how African community media is shifting from geographically-bound, general-interest outlets into mission-driven digital platforms — specialising in themes like gender justice and climate change. Radio and websites are now tied as the primary channel at 45% each, with outlets increasingly running hybrid models that combine FM broadcasts with podcasts and social media. Participation remains central to community media's identity, but sustainability now depends on collaboration and new monetisation approaches that close persistent digital-skills gaps.

1 August 2025 · Sandra Roberts

Q&A: How Radio NFM builds community on a limited budget

A case-study conversation with Radio NFM 98.1, a community station serving 150,000 people across South Africa's Namakwa region on the Northern Cape, broadcasting in four languages (70% Afrikaans, 15% Nama, 10% isiXhosa, 5% English). With no donor funding, the station relies on advertising, community fundraising and service initiatives — soup drives, back-to-school programmes — plus creative low-cost engagement like WhatsApp voice notes and face-to-face outreach weeks that remote broadcasting can't replicate. Programmes Manager Rose Giko: "community members bake cakes, share challenges" during those outreaches.

18 July 2025 · CivicSignal Team

Pandemic still disrupts African news media in 2025

The post that opened the #MediaMeltdown series: a survey of 42 community media outlets across 11 African countries found the pandemic's economic damage still unresolved five years on. Over a third of outlets saw advertising revenue fall more than 20%, and half of grant-dependent organisations lost more than 20% of their funding since 2020. Despite diversifying into training, events and commissioned content, many outlets report they still haven't recovered pre-2020 financial stability, or the ability to fund essential equipment and staffing.
