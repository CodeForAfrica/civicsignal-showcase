Africa's new media map is being redrawn. We are mapping it.

Code for Africa and SembraMedia ran the African expansion of Global Project Oasis: a systematic effort to identify, verify and profile independent digital-native media across seven diverse African countries — a first of its kind. CivicSignal builds on that foundation, extending the same mapping to other emerging media and tracking how it all changes over time.

The launch proposition

A first-of-its-kind mapping of Africa's digital-native media.

This is one of the first systematic efforts to map digital-native media across multiple African countries — spanning seven diverse markets, from Dakar to Nairobi. It's a genuine milestone for the sector, and Code for Africa is proud to have partnered with SembraMedia on it. CivicSignal builds on that foundation by extending the same mapping approach to other emerging media, and tracking how all of it evolves over time.

Six findings that carry the research

What the survey of 763 digital-native media organisations found.

01 · DISCOVERY

A fragmented media layer becomes visible

One of the first systematic efforts to map and analyse independent digital-native media across multiple African countries — the scale of discovery is the finding, not a footnote.

02 · FOUNDING WAVE

Almost 1 in 3 outlets founded 2019–2022

The pandemic and its aftermath coincided with a pronounced rise in digital-native formation — crisis disrupted legacy media, but also expanded demand for timely digital information. An association, not a proven single cause.

Founded 2019–2022 (≈33%)
03 · LANGUAGE

99% publish primarily in English, French or Arabic

English 64.4%, French 28.4%, Arabic 6.2%. Nearly half also use at least one additional language — including Swahili, Wolof, Hausa, isiXhosa, isiZulu, Yoruba and others — pointing to emerging multilingual practice.

English 64.4% French 28.4% Arabic 6.2% Other ≈1%
04 · BUSINESS MODEL

62.7% for-profit — but mostly small

The sector is increasingly formal and commercial, yet more than three-quarters of outlets reporting staff data have 20 or fewer workers. Formalisation doesn't automatically mean institutional security.

For-profit 62.7% Non-profit / other 37.3%
05 · LEADERSHIP

59.3% have men-only founding teams

Women founders are considerably more visible in non-profit and hybrid organisations (55.2% and 48.0%) than in for-profit ones (30.6%). Market-led growth alone won't fix this gap.

Men-only founding team 59.3% Mixed / women-only 40.7%
06 · INFLUENCE

Platform breadth predicts social reach

The number of active social platforms has the strongest association with an outlet's social-media influence score (Spearman ρ = 0.90). Team size and organisational age matter too.

Faces behind the data

What the numbers look like in seven different media markets.

A few illustrative organisations from the full report — chosen to show how differently the same forces (language, platform reach, business model) play out country to country.

Senegal

Seneweb — founded 1999, one of the earliest digital-natives in the study. 2.4M Facebook followers and 2M on Instagram; 35 full-time staff.

Morocco

El Botola — sports outlet founded 2007 on "0 dirham." Now commands 5.5M Facebook followers covering football across five languages.

Nigeria

Inclusive News Network — hybrid-funded outlet reporting on and for persons with disabilities, blending reader donations, ads and partnerships.

Ghana

AkwatiaTV publishes primarily in Twi — one of very few indigenous-language digital-natives identified in the entire sample.

Côte d'Ivoire

The country's non-profit digital-native sector is led largely by women, including Influence Mag, focused on empowering young girls and African women through education.

Kenya

Until Everyone Is Free — a podcast publishing exclusively in Sheng, a Kiswahili-English blend with no formalised writing system.

Reading these numbers responsibly

What to say, and what not to collapse.

Say this

  • Close to 1,100 digital-native organisations were identified; 763 were included in the Project Oasis research and directory.
  • Almost a third of surveyed organisations were founded between 2019 and 2022.
  • The founding wave coincided with the pandemic period — an association, not a proven sole cause.
  • 99% of surveyed outlets publish primarily in English, French or Arabic.
  • Platform breadth is the strongest predictor in the report's social-media influence model.

Avoid this

  • Collapsing the ≈1,100 identified outlets and the 763 included organisations into one denominator.
  • Claiming the pandemic caused every new outlet — the report supports a strong association, not a clean causal estimate.
  • Describing Project Oasis as a complete census of African digital-native media — it covers seven countries with documented response limitations.
  • Presenting follower totals as equivalent to trust, quality or civic value.
